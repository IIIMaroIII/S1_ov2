New ideas are exciting, but they must survive reality. That’s why the product must be a commercially viable proposition—not only innovative, but also sellable and scalable. Often, the idea sounded far-fetched at first; however, breakthroughs rarely look “safe” on day one. In fact, the research team finally made a breakthrough by testing, failing, and iterating, and then the reforms brought about improvements in efficiency that people could actually measure.

Innovation is also practical problem-solving. We found a way to get round a problem by reframing the constraint instead of fighting it. Still, execution requires resources: the company invests heavily in cutting-edge technology, and sometimes the investors have a pretty deep pockets, which enables speed. Nevertheless, money can distort priorities, so responsible leadership means being grounded in coree values.

Culture is the multiplier. Flexibility and trust are the hallmarks of a strong team.
