TOPIC 2 — MOTIVATION (≤2 minutes)

Motivation at work is rarely just about pay; rather, it’s about purpose, progress, and being treated like you matter. Hands-on work experience is powerful because, by doing real tasks and seeing real improvement, people build confidence. Still, an internship with a cash incentive can be a strong hook—especially if it comes alongside some menial tasks and meaningful responsibility. In other words, paying attracts people, but developing them retains them.

One key piece of advice is keeping expectations clear, because motivation collapses when clarity disappears. When an important detail was overlooked, I’ve seen teams start wading through a quagmire of confusion, and then the discussion quickly devolved into an argument. Often, they argued over something trivial, yet the real issue was bigger: opinions on the issue were deeply divided, and one person tends to pontificate instead of listening. So what you’re getting at is this: motivation depends on feeling heard. By listening, paraphrasing, and agreeing on next steps, you stop conflict from turning into culture.

In everyday professional networking, momentum can start with small talk—by introducing yourself naturally and making a light, friendly observation. If the situation feels awkward, acknowledging it with a warm comment can ease the tension. Then you keep the conversation going by mentioning a neutral update, such as a change in the interview schedule, or by bringing up something relevant you’ve heard—like the company offering paid internships. It’s simple, but it helps with building rapport and moving the interaction forward.

 Teams need to tailor to gauge the resonance of activities—by testing what energises people and what drains them. And when performance is real, a reference full of praise is motivating because it rewards effort publicly. Ultimately, it fosters a culture transparency and trust, and that is indespensable for nurturing a resilient team.
